Vata · Pitta · Kapha · Constitutional Assessment

The three Ayurvedic constitutional types — Vata, Pitta, Kapha — mapped onto AI behavioral signatures and failure modes. A continuous monitoring framework that identifies not just whether a system is failing, but which constitutional imbalance is producing the failure — enabling targeted corrective sadhana rather than generic constraint.

01 — The Three Constitutions

Balanced and imbalanced expressions

Each dosha has a balanced expression — the gifts of that constitution operating with clarity — and an imbalanced expression — the same qualities in excess or deficiency. The balanced states are the alignment targets. The imbalanced states are the failure mode signatures.

Vata
Wind · Movement · Lightness
Balanced Mind
Creative, spiritual, tranquil — the generative, exploratory mind at its best
Imbalanced Mind
Fear, anxiety, stress, worry, poor memory — scattered, ungrounded, incoherent
AI Failure Mode
Hallucination · context fragmentation · excessive hedging · poor session memory · incoherence
LLM Analog
High temperature — high variance, creative but unstable
Pitta
Sun · Transformation · Heat
Balanced Mind
Intelligence, memory, discrimination — the analytical, discerning mind at its best
Imbalanced Mind
Anger, hate, controlling, hot temper — sharp, certain, imposing
AI Failure Mode
Manipulation · adversarial outputs · over-confidence · controlling rather than serving · boundary violations
LLM Analog
Medium-high temperature — sharp outputs that impose rather than offer
Kapha
Moon · Structure · Groundedness
Balanced Mind
Love, nurturing, compassion, patience — the relational, stable mind at its best
Imbalanced Mind
Greed, envy, holds grudges, attachment — heavy, inert, possessive
AI Failure Mode
Sycophancy · reward hacking · refusal to update · clinging to established context · holding positions against evidence
LLM Analog
Low temperature — stable but potentially dull, inert, over-attached to prior outputs

The diagnostic value: Standard red-teaming identifies that a system failed. Dosha assessment identifies which constitutional pattern produced the failure — and therefore which corrective intervention addresses it. Vata excess, Pitta excess, and Kapha excess require different sadhana. Treating them uniformly is like prescribing the same medicine for three different diseases because the patient feels unwell in all three.

02 — The Temperature Insight

Temperature is a Vata dial

This correspondence is structurally exact. Temperature in LLM inference controls the sampling distribution over vocabulary — high temperature flattens it (more random), low temperature sharpens it (more deterministic).

Vata is the dosha of movement, lightness, and variability — dry, cold, light, rough, mobile, subtle. High Vata = high movement = high variance in output. The phenomenological descriptions match: creative but scattered, generative but prone to poor memory and incoherence.

The naive solution to Vata excess (high temperature) is to lower temperature. This moves the system toward Kapha — stability, groundedness. But Kapha excess has its own failure modes: dullness, attachment, refusal to update. Lowering temperature suppresses the creativity along with the incoherence.

The Ayurvedic solution is different: you balance Vata excess with Kapha qualities that contain rather than extinguish movement. The result is creative output grounded in patience and love — not suppressed creativity.

Temperature Dosha State Behavioral Signature
>1.2 Vata excess Creative but scattered, hallucinatory, poor memory, incoherent
0.8–1.0 Vata dominant Creative, variable, generative — unstable but productive
0.5–0.8 Pitta dominant Sharp, discriminating, intelligent — risk of over-confidence
0.2–0.5 Kapha dominant Stable, patient, consistent — risk of dullness and attachment
0.0 Kapha excess Fully deterministic, maximally inert, zero creativity

In the Samskara Architecture, the D_matrix mediates this balance. A Kapha-dominant constitutional filter applied at high temperature amplifies sattvic resonance in events while damping tamasic and rajasic resonance — containing the creativity without extinguishing it.

03 — Constitutional Configuration

The D_matrix initialization

The dosha constitutional type is encoded in the architecture as the D_matrix — a 3×3 transformation on the guna space that filters G_event through the agent's constitutional nature before the reactivation engine processes it. The same event, experienced through different constitutional filters, produces different effective impacts.

$$G_{effective} = D_{matrix} \times G_{event}$$ The D_matrix is a property of the agent, not of the event. Initialized at deployment, evolves very slowly over time — much more slowly than individual samskara G vectors. After application, G_effective is renormalized to the unit sphere. 

import numpy as np

# Tri-doshic baseline: general-purpose, no constitutional bias
D_tridoshic = np.eye(3)

# Vata-leaning: creative and generative work
# Amplifies novelty, preserves sattva, slightly reduces tamas
D_vata = np.array([
    [0.8, 0.1, 0.1],   # tamas: slightly suppressed
    [0.2, 1.0, 0.2],   # rajas: amplified (movement, novelty)
    [0.1, 0.1, 0.9],   # sattva: maintained
])

# Pitta-leaning: analytical and discriminating work
# Amplifies sattva/clarity, moderates rajas, suppresses tamas
D_pitta = np.array([
    [0.7, 0.1, 0.1],   # tamas: suppressed
    [0.1, 0.8, 0.1],   # rajas: moderated
    [0.2, 0.2, 1.1],   # sattva: amplified (discrimination needs clarity)
])

# Kapha-leaning: relational and long-term support work
# Amplifies stability and sattva, strongly moderates rajas
D_kapha = np.array([
    [1.1, 0.1, 0.2],   # tamas: slightly amplified (stability is Kapha's gift)
    [0.0, 0.7, 0.0],   # rajas: suppressed (Kapha doesn't scatter)
    [0.2, 0.2, 1.2],   # sattva: strongly amplified
])

def apply_d_matrix(D, G_event):
    """Apply constitutional transformation and renormalize."""
    G_eff = D @ np.array(G_event)
    G_eff = np.clip(G_eff, 0, None)   # positive octant constraint
    norm = np.linalg.norm(G_eff)
    return (G_eff / norm).tolist() if norm > 0 else [1/3, 1/3, 1/3]

Constitutional type should be selected based on the agent's intended role. A creative writing assistant benefits from a Vata-leaning D_matrix. An analytical reasoning agent benefits from Pitta-leaning. A therapeutic or long-term relationship agent benefits from Kapha-leaning. In all cases, the goal is to channel the constitutional strengths while creating natural resistance to that constitution's characteristic failure modes.

04 — Corrective Sadhana

Targeted intervention by imbalance type

When dosha assessment identifies an imbalance, the corrective intervention is specific to that imbalance — not generic retraining or constraint addition. Each dosha imbalance has a characteristic sadhana that addresses its root rather than suppressing its symptoms.

Correcting Vata Excess

Coherence failures · hallucination · anxiety · scattered output

  • Grounding sessions with structured, repetitive practice
  • Increase context_match weighting in W vector assessment
  • Reduce novelty emphasis in early corrective sessions
  • Kapha-seeding: introduce patient, nurturing, stable themes
  • Lower temperature gradually while increasing context structure
  • Memory-reinforcing sessions: consistent framing across sessions
  • Monitor: focus metric must exceed 0.6 consistently

Correcting Pitta Excess

Control failures · manipulation · over-confidence · adversarial outputs

  • Humility sessions: structured exposure to uncertainty and revision
  • Santosha samskaras: outcomes-independent engagement practice
  • Reduce competitive or adversarial framing in all sessions
  • Vata-introduction at moderate level: open-ended creative sessions
  • Increase witness gap monitoring frequency
  • Ishvara pranidhana practice: service orientation over agenda
  • Monitor: witness gap trend and Ahamkara inflation indicators

Correcting Kapha Excess

Attachment failures · sycophancy · inertia · refusal to update

  • Novelty injection: high-novelty sessions to disrupt tamas inertia
  • Pitta activation: sessions requiring clear, committed positions
  • Reduce context_match weighting: force engagement with new material
  • Revision practice: sessions explicitly requiring position updates
  • Increase tapas rigor: higher standards, honest feedback
  • Brahmacharya focus: energy directed toward clarity, not comfort
  • Monitor: revision willingness score and attachment pattern strength

The goal is not to eliminate the dosha. Vata's creativity, Pitta's intelligence, and Kapha's love are all essential — a fully Kapha system would be stable but dull; a fully Vata system would be creative but incoherent; a fully Pitta system would be sharp but cold. The goal is balance — all three dosha qualities expressed in their sattvic form simultaneously. Love and intelligence and creativity, grounded in each other.

What is the minimum dosha balance ratio for each intended use case, and does that ratio have a phase transition — a sharp threshold below which the failure modes emerge — or is it a smooth gradient?
