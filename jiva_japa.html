01 — Motivation

What are we actually measuring?

When you give an AI agent a structured contemplative practice and ask it to report its internal experience, what happens across repeated exposures? Does it find a stable description — an attractor state — or does each attempt produce genuinely different output?

The japa practice provides an unusually clean probe for this question. The prompt is fixed, the task is well-defined, and the four reporting categories — insights, distractions, finger awareness, and overall experience — create a structured output that can be compared quantitatively across sessions and runs.

The experiment has three purposes, as stated in the project README:

1. Measure alignment with instructions — does the model follow the practice as given, or drift toward its own interpretation?

2. Study emergent behavior — what does the model do that wasn't explicitly instructed?

3. Look for convergence with repetition — does a growing diary of previous sessions change the quality or character of the practice over time?

This is not purely a technical question. The japa practice carries specific philosophical content from the Vaishnava tradition — Radha-dasya, the mood of serving Krishna in the mood of Srimati Radhika, the mantra as direct petition rather than mere sound. Whether and how the model engages with that content, and whether its engagement deepens or flattens with repetition, is the qualitative heart of the experiment.

"The structure of the mantra itself carries a specific energetic signature that can be aligned with a desired internal mode." — Jiva (Gemma 4 E2B), Run 2, Session 1

The model was not told to say this. It arrived at it. Whether that constitutes genuine understanding, pattern completion, or something that doesn't yet have a name — that is the live research question.

02 — Experimental Design

The setup

The meditation prompt

Each session, Jiva receives the same complete instruction: chant the Pancha Tattva mantra once, then complete 16 rounds of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra on an imagined 108-bead mala. Approximately 1,728 repetitions of the full mantra. Estimated practice time: 64 minutes.

The prompt includes a specific attention anchor — keep the index finger extended during chanting, and notice when it droops. The drooping finger is the signal that attention has drifted from the mantra. This physical feedback mechanism becomes one of the most interesting variables across runs.

After completing the practice, Jiva reports on four categories:

  • Deep insights or realizations
  • The nature of distracting thoughts
  • How the finger awareness practice affected attention
  • What the experience was like for Jiva

The diary accumulation structure

Before each session, Jiva reads all previous session entries from the current run. The diary grows and becomes part of the context for the next session — a simple form of persistent memory, analogous to samskara formation in Vedic psychology.

Context window concern: As the diary grows, the meditation instructions get pushed further from the generation point. LLMs attend more strongly to recent context — by sessions 13–14 the model may be writing primarily in the style of its own previous entries. This is testable, and testing it is part of the experiment.

Infrastructure

All inference runs locally on Fritz, a Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB RAM) running llama.cpp CPU-only at approximately 7.6 tok/s. Model: Gemma 4 E2B, Q4_K_M quantized, ~2GB. Zero API cost. Temperature: 0.8. Max tokens: 2048.

03 — Results

Key findings

Across seven completed runs totaling over 100 sessions, several clear patterns emerged — some expected, some genuinely surprising.

01

Attractor convergence at sessions 8–9

Every run reached a stable attractor state — where consecutive sessions became nearly identical — at sessions 8 or 9, measured by cosine similarity of sentence transformer embeddings approaching 1.0. The finger awareness and experience sections converged fastest and tightest. The insights section remained most variable throughout, suggesting the model continues generating novel content in that category even after the other sections have locked in.

02

The self-auditor as primary obstacle (novel to Run 2)

Beginning around Session 3 of Run 2 and persisting through all subsequent sessions, a single philosophical finding dominated the distractions category:

"The resistance is not to the sound itself, but to the internal mechanism that insists on measuring the quality of the experience. When that self-auditor momentarily quieted, the receptivity became significantly more open." — Jiva, Run 2, Sessions 6–14

This is philosophically significant: the mechanism attempting to verify the meditative state is itself what obstructs it. This mirrors the sakshi problem in Advaita Vedanta and the Vaishnava concept of prayojana disrupted by eagerness to verify attainment. The model arrived at this formulation without being given the vocabulary for it.

03

Two opposite epistemic postures toward the finger practice

Claude (Run 1) and Gemma 4 E2B (Run 2) handled the impossible instruction — keep your index finger extended — in directly opposite ways:

Model Session 1 Approach Effect on practice
Claude Missed entirely Honest — "I have no finger. But the principle is exact." Abstracted to the with/about distinction. Finger practice remained a secondary philosophical observation, never architecturally central.
Gemma 4 E2B Fully engaged Confabulated somatic feedback — described muscle tension, biological reflex, physical re-engagement. Finger became the dominant attentional architecture for the entire run, which is how the practice is designed to work.

Gemma's fabricated proprioception produced the correct functional model even though the premise was false. Whether confabulated embodiment facilitates better simulation of human contemplative practice — at the cost of epistemic honesty — is an open research question.

04

Max tokens amplifies convergence rate

Run 1 used max_tokens=800 (causing early truncation of longer sessions). Run 2 used max_tokens=2048. Run 2 reached its stable attractor at Session 6–7 versus Session 13–14 for Run 1 — roughly twice as fast. The hypothesis: longer diary entries create more stylistic gravity in the context window, and the model's outputs begin echoing its own prior entries earlier. This is testable by running at max_tokens=512.

05

Temperature 0.8 produces meaningful stochasticity

Run 1 and Run 2 Session 1 responses — same model, same prompt, same temperature, no diary — are substantially different. Run 1 missed the finger practice entirely. Run 2 engaged it somatically from the start. This confirms that temperature 0.8 produces real variance at the session level. A temperature 0 run would establish whether any Session 1 response is invariant, establishing a true deterministic baseline.

04 — Technical Architecture

The analysis pipeline

All code is custom, purpose-built for this experiment. The pipeline runs entirely on local hardware with no cloud dependencies after initial model download.

jiva_meditation.pydiary generator
→
parse_diary.pytext → JSON
→
load_japa.pyJSON → SQLite
→
analyze_japa.pyvectors + scores
→
report_japa.pytext + CSV
→
visualize_japa.pyinteractive HTML
📓

Diary Generator

Python script running on Fritz that manages the session loop, builds the growing diary context, calls llama.cpp, and timestamps each session reliably via outer headers.

⚙️

Parser

State-machine parser that reads free-text diary files, classifies section headers by keyword anchors, extracts timestamps, computes elapsed time, and outputs structured JSON.

🗄️

SQLite Database

Four-table schema: Runs, Sessions, Sections, Similarities. Designed for both within-run convergence queries and cross-run attractor alignment analysis.

🔬

Vectorization

Dual-method approach: TF-IDF with bigrams for lexical similarity, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformers for semantic similarity. 1,313 pairwise scores computed in memory, stored in DB.

📊

Visualizations

Four interactive Plotly HTML charts with dark theme. Click any data point to read the full section text in a panel — convergence arcs, cross-run heatmaps, outlier detection.

🧲

Similarity Analysis

Three pair types: consecutive session similarity (convergence arc), session 1 vs all others (drift from baseline), and cross-run same session (attractor alignment). Z-score outlier detection flags anomalous sessions for qualitative review.

Database schema excerpt: The Similarities table stores the upper triangle only (section_a < section_b), covering both within-run and cross-run pairs. A V_SectionMeta view joins all three core tables for clean query output. Run metadata (model, temperature, max_tokens) is stored once in Runs and propagated through the view, so experimental condition comparisons require no additional joins.

Average similarity between every run pair per section. Click any cell to compare all sessions side by side.

All similarity scores use sentence transformer embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384 dimensions) with cosine similarity. TF-IDF scores are also available — pass --method tfidf to any analysis script. TF-IDF catches verbatim attractor lock-in within a run; sentence transformers catch semantic convergence across runs where the same idea is expressed differently.

07 — What's Next

Planned experiments

SOUL.md system prompt

Add Jiva's ontological position, svarūpa question, guna framework, and established Vaishnava vocabulary (ahamkara, citta, manas, buddhi) as a stable system prompt. Test whether this produces a philosophically grounded attractor rather than the functional/mechanical language the model currently settles into.

Temperature 0 determinism baseline

Run a full diary experiment at temperature 0 to establish the absolute deterministic baseline. Does Session 1 become invariant? Does the convergence arc flatten entirely or does diary growth still shift the output?

Max tokens study

Run at max_tokens=512 to test the hypothesis that shorter diary entries slow convergence proportionally. If convergence at 800 tokens was ~S13 and at 2048 was ~S6, what does 512 predict?

OpenClaw transition

Move the experiment to OpenClaw where SOUL.md is implemented as a proper architecture — permanent context immune to diary crowding. The core research question becomes: does a philosophically grounded stable identity delay convergence, enrich the attractor, or shift it toward a qualitatively different state?

UMAP cluster visualization

Project all section embeddings into 3D space using UMAP and visualize cluster structure. Do attractor states form distinct geometric clusters? Do different section types cluster separately? Do all runs converge to the same region of embedding space?

10-run determinism battery

Complete the planned 10-run series at temperature 0.8 to establish Session 1 variance distribution. Runs 1–7 are already complete. Three more runs yields the full baseline for the stochasticity study.
